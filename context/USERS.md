## Interview Synthesis

### Interview 1
- **Who (role or relationship, no full name needed):** For my first interview, I interviewed a first-year student at Dragonfly Aviation, a flight school in Winder, GA. He is aspiring to be a commercial pilot and is currently working towards his private pilot's license (PPL) while also working at the front desk of the school.
- **Situation I asked about:** All of my questions centered around his response to the first one: Tell me about a situation where you felt like you didn't belong or weren't as qualified as the people around you? He described feeling this way at the flight school where he works. Several of his coworkers have already completed their check rides or are qualified to begin CFI training, while he hasn't even earned his PPL yet. Although he is capable of doing his job duties--checking people in, tying down planes, dispatching the iPads, etc.--he feels less qualified than his coworkers because of his lack of pilot experience and inability to contribute to conversations about flying to the same extent of those around him. In college, he was an accomplished collegiate athlete, and going from being very confident in his position to not having much faith in himself was somewhat of a shock to his system.
- **What surprised me:** One thing that surprised me was that he attributed prior poor performance to his current situation. He admitted to not being the strongest student in college and having poor study habits. Because of his past performance, he already feels like he isn't going to be able to become a pilot, despite saying he's putting in 10x the effort he did previously. Another thing that surprised me was that he put an "expiration date" on this feeling. While I did not prompt him towards any future intentions, he declared that he felt like he was going to feel this way until he passed his first check ride because that would prove to himself that he's an actual pilot and can do this. Additionally, I expected his imposter syndrome to be primarily related to his performance in flight training, but instead his answer focused more on workplace and  relationships with his coworkers. His lack of experience didn't seem to bother him as much just because he was behind but, instead, became more significant when this perceived gap in ability was socially visible around his coworkers.

### Interview 2
- **Who:** My second interview was with a first-year female business student at Georgia Tech who was deferred from early action admission and chose to drop out of sorority recruitment.
- **Situation I asked about:** In this interview, I asked her to describe a time when she felt like an imposter and what caused those feelings. She described feeling this way when she first got into Tech and while adjusting to the social environment at school. She felt like an imposter after being deferred because it made her feel like she was not as qualified as the students who were accepted early. Once she got to Tech, she also felt out of place when her roommate and floormates attended sorority events or brought their new friends around.
- **What surprised me:** What surprised me was that she would point out evidence that she was qualified/accepted and still feel like she did not belong. I was also surprised that her feelings came less from being excluded and more from feeling like she did not have a comparable experience. She wished she had events to attend/people to bring back because it felt like the expectation, even though she wasn't in these environments. I was most surprised that these feelings did not necessarily cause her to make a major change. She said she attempted and then just gave up.
